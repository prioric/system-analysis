# Alder Pipes grew for five years on Westland's small lots, and Westland's next program comes in lots larger than any Alder has carried

Status: draft, not checked by a person
Evidence current as of: 10 November 2026

## 1. In short

**Alder Pipes earns 46% of its revenue from one water board, Westland, and Westland's new program has no lot Alder has the guarantee or the cash to carry alone.**

Alder's board of directors decides by June 2027, when the first three Westland lots open, how Alder bids for them. It can bid alone, join a larger builder as the junior partner, or stay with the small lots at the six smaller boards. Each choice has a price, and a different party pays it.

## 2. The market

**Seven water boards spend a steady €1.1 billion a year on pipe renewal, and Alder is the smallest of the four builders that win most of it.**

Alder Pipes lays water and gas pipes for public utilities in one country. It sells to seven regional water boards and two gas network operators, through public tenders, against Hollis Build, Carrow Group and Birch Civil. The board that uses the pipes is the same body that pays for the work.

Westland is the largest board and spends about a third of the total. Its share of Alder's revenue rose from 21% in 2021 to 46% in 2025. The six smaller boards paid 41% and the gas operators 13%. In October 2026 Westland published its program for 2027 to 2037, with lots of €60 million and more.

## 3. The rules of the trade

**A board buys on the lowest price backed by a bank guarantee, and pays months after the builder has paid its crews.**

Each lot goes to the lowest price that comes with a bank guarantee of a tenth of the lot. Past performance is a gate: twenty years of good work and two years both pass it, and after that only price and guarantee count. The builder pays wages, pipe and machines from the first month. The board pays within 60 days of accepting a section, and a section takes four to six months, so every builder carries months of its own spending on each lot.

Figures an outsider would misread:

- Receivables of 31% of revenue: in this trade a quarter to a third of revenue at year end is normal, because the boards accept most sections in autumn.
- Twenty years of acceptance records: the record passes the gate and adds nothing after it.

## 4. Who works with whom

**Alder competes with the same three builders for lots and for welders, and could also become the junior partner of one of them.**

Hollis Build is the largest builder, with revenue of €920 million in 2025, against Alder's €84 million. Hollis Build and Carrow Group hire licensed welders from the same two training schools as Alder, so a good year for all of them is a tight year for welders. The rules let builders bid as a consortium and split the guarantee by share, so a competitor on one lot can be Alder's partner on the next. Birch Civil, closest to Alder in size, keeps its largest buyer at 18% of its revenue.

## 5. What Alder has and what limits it

**Alder has crews the boards trust, and its guarantee and cash have not grown with its work.**

Alder employs its own 410 welders and pipe layers, and the smaller boards have recorded fewer defects on its sections than their average for five years. Its largest completed contract was €38 million, with a guarantee of €3.8 million, the largest it has posted. Its receivables rose from 19% of revenue in 2021 to 31% in 2025, while its cash stayed at 9%, or €7.6 million.

The annual report calls Alder "the specialist for the boards' renewal decade". Its bid list for 2026 holds no lot over €40 million.

## 6. Where the facts pull against each other

**The buyer that gave Alder its growth now buys in a size Alder has never carried, and a larger lot would stretch the gap between paying and being paid past Alder's cash.**

### One buyer, one new program

Alder earns 46% of its revenue from Westland, and Westland's smallest new lot of €60 million is larger than any contract Alder has completed.

Alder's four open Westland contracts, worth €61 million together, finish between 2027 and 2029. The bid manager is paid for each lot won, with no weight on size, so the pay rewards the small lots that are running out.

Who pays: the crews on Westland work and the Alder family's holding, whose largest buyer has no next job for Alder after 2029 unless the board changes how Alder bids by June 2027.

### Paid late, paying early

Alder pays its crews every month and is paid after each section is accepted, and a €60 million lot would widen that gap past the cash Alder has.

The guarantee on such a lot would be €6 million, against €7.6 million of cash at the end of 2025. Kestrel Bank charges its fee on the guarantee from the first day, while the first payment comes months later.

Who pays: Alder Holding, whose cash would carry the guarantee and the unpaid work together from the first month of the lot.

## 7. The decision

**Alder's board of directors decides by June 2027 how Alder bids for Westland's first three lots.**

- **Alder bids alone on the smallest Westland lot.** It uses its own crews and its own balance sheet. **Cost:** a bank guarantee of €6 million and months of unpaid work, carried by Alder Holding and its cash.
- **Alder joins a consortium as the junior partner that lays the pipe.** Hollis Build or Carrow Group would lead and post most of the guarantee. **Cost:** the lead builder sets the price and keeps the margin on the whole lot, carried by Alder's profit.
- **Alder keeps bidding on lots under €40 million at the six smaller boards.** **Cost:** the Westland work, 46% of revenue in 2025, which runs out as the open lots finish, carried by the crews on Westland work.

Bidding alone and joining a consortium cannot run on the same lot, because Westland bars a firm from two bids on one lot. Either can run beside the small lots at the smaller boards.

If nobody decides, the bid list stays under €40 million and the third option happens by default.

Two dates will show which way it goes: Alder's half-year accounts in September 2027, which show whether a guarantee was posted, and Westland's award list in December 2027.

## 8. Limits and sources

The figures come from Alder's annual reports for 2021 to 2025, the Westland tender notice of 3 October 2026, Westland's general conditions of January 2024, and the 2025 annual reports of Hollis Build, Carrow Group and Birch Civil. No filing gives Kestrel Bank's guarantee limit for Alder, so whether Alder could post €6 million at all is unknown. The company and every figure here are invented for this example.
