# The first reading

One Markdown page, `{company}/reading.md`, cut from the ten chapters. It tells one story in eight parts, market first. Write it from `{skill}/assets/reading_template.md`.

## The top of the page

```
# {One sentence that states what is going on at the company.}

Status: draft, not checked by a person
Evidence current as of: {date}
```

The title is a full sentence a stranger understands on one reading. It says what is going on and who it matters to.

## The eight parts

Each heading starts with its number, `## 1.` to `## 8.`, followed by the part's name in the reading's language. Parts 1 to 7 open on one bold sentence that states what the part finds. Then plain paragraphs of three sentences at most. Write no sentence that only leads into the next part.

1. **In short.** What is going on, in three to five sentences. The decision, and the body that makes it, by when.
2. **The market.** The first paragraph says what the company sells, to whom, how, where and against whom. Then who buys, how much, and what changed this year. From the Context and Customers chapters.
3. **The rules of the trade.** How work is won and how money moves, in plain words, from the Sector rules chapter. Then a short list of the figures an outsider would misread, each with the reason.
4. **Who works with whom.** The buyers, the partners, the shared suppliers and the competitors. Name what each set of competitors competes for. Name any firm that is a partner on one job and a competitor on the next.
5. **What the company has and what limits it.** The strongest asset and the binding limits, as plain facts. Where the company says one thing and does another, say both, with their sources.
6. **Where the facts pull against each other.** Two or three pulls. Each is a `### ` heading with a short title, then:
   - one sentence that states both facts and the pull between them;
   - a short paragraph on how the two facts meet;
   - `Who pays:` and who carries the cost of the pull, by when.
   A pull is a problem the head of the company would recognize as theirs today. It rests on two sourced facts. Swap a competitor's name in: when the sentence stays true, it is too general, and it goes.
7. **The decision.** The body that decides and the date by which it must decide. Then each option as a bullet:
   `- **{The option, in the third person, as a fact.}** {What it means.} **Cost:** {what it costs}, carried by {who}.`
   Then which options can run at the same time and which cannot. Then what happens if nobody decides. Then the dates and filings that will show which way it goes.
8. **Limits and sources.** The main sources the reader can open, with their dates. The limits a reader needs: a figure resting on one source, two sources that disagree, a pull that is an inference.

## How to write it

- One idea per sentence, at most one figure per sentence, each sentence under 45 words.
- Keep a cause and its result in one sentence, joined by "so", "because" or "while". A list of short facts with the causes cut out reads as random data.
- Say how each fact connects to the one before: who pays whom, who leads, what depends on what.
- Take every figure from a chapter Evidence bullet, in the same form. The check refuses a number that sits in no sourced chapter line.
- State each fact once, in the part where it first matters.
- No lists of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities or threats. Those headings score the company. Part 5 states facts, and part 6 shows each pull with who pays.
- In another language, write in that language from the chapters. Never translate an English draft. Use the trade's own words. The labels come from `setup.md`.
