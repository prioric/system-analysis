# The ten chapters

A chapter rebuilds how one part of the company works, from the evidence alone. It carries facts with sources and dates. It carries no conclusion and no advice. Use any framework you know silently: ask its questions, and never name it in the file.

## The ten files

| File | What it rebuilds |
|---|---|
| `01_context.md` | The world the company did not choose: its industry, markets, size and growth, regulation, technology shifts, and what changed in the last year. For a listed company, how the stock market values it beside named competitors, as a dated fact. |
| `02_customers.md` | Who buys, group by group: what each group uses the product for, who decides, who pays, how often, and what it costs them to switch. Under `### The split`, a table of the buyer groups with each group's share of revenue where a filing gives one. Where the user and the payer are different actors, name both and split them. Show what buyers are seen to do: renewals, tender awards, reviews, churn. A seller's claim about its own customers is a claim, and the chapter says who made it. |
| `03_competition.md` | Everyone who wins the same customer job: direct competitors, other ways to meet the same need, and anyone who bids for the same scarce input. Name the **floor**: the buyer doing nothing, waiting, or keeping what they have. A peer table of revenue, growth and margin for the named competitors. |
| `04_assets.md` | What the company owns: technology, patents, brand, relationships, data, people, capital, sites, licenses, scale. For each, say in plain words how long it is likely to last and what shows it. |
| `05_capabilities.md` | What the company does again and again, with evidence. An asset is owned; a capability is done. For each, say how long it is likely to last and what shows it. |
| `06_limitations.md` | What binds the company: debts, claims on its cash, governance locks, regulatory ceilings, things it cannot do. |
| `07_operating_model.md` | How it is run: ownership, capital structure, who decides what, how people are paid, how fast decisions are made. Under `### Who decides`, one line per body that can act (the board, the owner, a regulator, a buyer's committee) and what it controls. |
| `08_capital_moves.md` | What it does with its money, in three parts: **Invests** (spending on plant, acquisitions, research, new markets) · **Cuts** (sales of units, closures, layoffs, exits) · **Hires** (hiring patterns, key appointments, new functions). |
| `09_narrative.md` | How it explains itself: why it exists, how it works, what it offers, what the buyer gets. What its words say, and what its pictures and website lead with. Say what each is about. End with two lines, `Narrative:` and `Signal:`, one sentence each. |
| `10_sector_rules.md` | What an insider in this trade knows that changes how the company's figures read: how work is won, who pays whom and when, what a normal balance sheet and a normal margin look like here, and which public records hold the truth. Under `### Figures that read differently here`, one bullet per figure an outsider would misread: the figure, the outsider's reading, the insider's reading, and the source. |

## The form

Every chapter has the same five sections:

```
# {Company} · {Chapter name}

## Summary
{4 to 6 sentences: how this part of the company works. Only facts from the Evidence below.}

## Evidence
- {One fact, with its figure or quoted line.} [{source}, {date}]
- …

## Confidence
{High, medium or low, and one line why. Name the weak claims.}

## Open questions
- {What you would need to know.}

## Unknowns
- {What the evidence does not cover.}
```

Every Evidence bullet ends on its source in square brackets, with a year or a full date: `[Alder annual report 2025, p. 22, April 2026]`. Mark an estimate `[ESTIMATE]` and an inference `[INFERRED]` after the source. Sub-headings with `###` inside Evidence are allowed.

A chapter that stays thin because the sources are thin says so in one line under Unknowns, and the run goes on.

## Observed behavior of institutional buyers

A consumer leaves reviews, ratings and app-store traffic. A hospital, a government or a business leaves none of those. For an institutional buyer, read instead: public tender awards with their terms and end dates; registers of what was actually chosen; the buyers' own reports that name their suppliers; regulators' inspection and recall data.

## Quick and deep runs

A quick run fills every chapter from fewer sources. Each chapter still has its five sections and at least three Evidence bullets. A deep run reads further and fills the open questions.
